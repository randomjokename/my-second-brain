Prompt #1
🎁

The Gift List Tamer

The problem

You have 23 people to buy for, a budget that's already stretched, and zero inspiration. Your Amazon cart has 47 items and you can't remember who any of them are for.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me get my holiday gift shopping organized and under control. People I need to buy for: [list each person with their relationship to you, age, and any interests you know] Budget per person (roughly): [amount or range] Total budget I can't exceed: [amount] Shopping preference: [online only / in-store / mix of both] Gift deadline: [date — when does everything need to be purchased by?] Give me: - An organized gift list with 2-3 ideas per person at different price points - Creative ideas for the "impossible to buy for" people - Group gift suggestions where it makes sense - A running budget tracker - Which gifts to order online NOW (shipping deadlines) vs buy in-store later
💡
Why it works

It turns the overwhelming mental list into an organized plan AND gives creative ideas you wouldn't think of when you're stress-shopping at 11pm.

Prompt #2
🍽️

The Holiday Menu Planner

The problem

You're hosting. Everyone has dietary restrictions. Aunt Carol expects her traditions. You want to actually enjoy the meal instead of spending 2 days in the kitchen crying into a casserole dish.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me plan the holiday meal without losing my mind. Number of guests: [number] Dietary needs/restrictions: [vegetarian aunt, nut allergy, gluten-free cousin, etc.] Must-have traditional dishes: [the non-negotiables — grandma's stuffing, etc.] My cooking skill level: [beginner / competent / confident] How much prep time I have: [days before, morning of, etc.] Budget for food: [amount] What stressed me out last time: [timing, too many dishes, oven space, etc.] Give me: - A complete menu that accommodates everyone - A prep-ahead timeline (what to make 3 days before, 1 day before, morning of) - A day-of cooking schedule with exact oven times so nothing overlaps - A shopping list organized by store section - One thing I can buy pre-made without guilt to save my sanity
💡
Why it works

The prep-ahead timeline is the game-changer. It spreads the work across days so you're not a zombie on the actual holiday. And "one thing to buy pre-made" gives you permission to take a shortcut.

Prompt #3
📅

The Holiday Schedule Negotiator

The problem

Three family dinners, two school concerts, one office party, and somehow you need to fit in actual holiday magic for your kids. December is a logistical nightmare and you're already exhausted just looking at the calendar.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me survive the holiday calendar without burning out. All commitments and events: [list everything — family dinners, school events, work parties, kids' activities, travel, etc.] Travel requirements: [who, where, how far, dates] Things I actually WANT to do: [the stuff that brings joy, not obligation] My energy level right now: [running on fumes / okay / actually good] Who I need to keep happy: [be honest — in-laws, boss, kids, etc.] Give me: - A realistic schedule that includes everything I truly can't miss - What I should consider declining — and a graceful script for each - Buffer days with NOTHING scheduled - Where to combine events or batch errands - Self-care windows (even 30 minutes counts)
💡
Why it works

The "what to decline" category is the real gift. Sometimes you need an objective voice telling you it's okay to skip your coworker's ugly sweater party.

Prompt #4
✍️

The Holiday Card Writer

The problem

You want to send cards but the thought of writing 40 personalized messages makes you want to skip it entirely. "Happy Holidays, love us" feels insufficient but you're not a poet.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me write holiday card messages that feel personal but don't take me 3 hours. I'm writing to: [list people or groups — close friends, extended family, neighbors, kids' teachers, etc.] A memory or thing I want to mention for each: [even something small — "loved seeing you at soccer," "your garden was amazing this year"] Our family's year highlights: [big moments — new baby, new job, trip, milestone] Tone I want: [funny / warm / heartfelt / mix] Any sensitive situations: [someone going through a divorce, lost a loved one, etc.] Give me a personalized message for each person/group. Keep them: - 2-4 sentences (not a novel) - Warm but not cheesy - Different enough that they don't all sound the same - Extra gentle for anyone going through something hard
💡
Why it works

It captures your voice and real memories — so the cards feel genuinely personal, not template-y, but took 1/10th the time.

Prompt #5
😤

The Family Drama Deflector

The problem

Every year, THAT conversation happens. Politics at the dinner table. Passive-aggressive comments about your parenting. The in-law who always goes too far. You want to enjoy the holiday, not referee a cage match.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me prepare for the family dynamics that always derail the holidays. The people involved: [who tends to cause tension and their relationship to you] The topics that always blow up: [politics, parenting choices, money, weight, relationships, etc.] My boundaries this year: [what you're NOT willing to tolerate] What I want to protect: [kids from arguments, your own peace, partner's feelings, etc.] The specific comment I'm dreading: [that one thing someone always says] Give me: - 3 topic-change scripts I can use when things start going sideways - A calm boundary phrase for when someone crosses the line - An exit strategy (how to leave a conversation or the event gracefully) - A pre-game plan to align with my partner before we arrive - One mantra I can repeat to myself when I want to lose it
💡
Why it works

Having literal scripts ready means you don't freeze or react in the moment. The "pre-game with partner" piece means you're a team, not two people separately drowning.

Prompt #6
✨

The Holiday Magic Maker

The problem

You want the season to feel special for your kids, but Pinterest has ruined your expectations, your budget is tight, and the elf on the shelf is starting to feel like a second job. You're performing "holiday magic" but not actually feeling any of it.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me create holiday magic that doesn't burn me out. Kids' ages: [ages] Budget for holiday extras: [amount] My bandwidth right now: [low / medium / high] Traditions we already love: [the ones that actually bring joy] Things that feel like obligations: [elf on the shelf, elaborate advent calendar, matching pajamas, etc.] What "holiday magic" means to my family: [togetherness / surprise / giving / coziness / religious meaning] Give me: - 5-7 simple daily or weekly traditions that match our values (not Pinterest's) - A "magic calendar" of small moments spread across the season - Free or low-cost ideas that feel more special than expensive ones - Which current obligations to DROP (with permission to let them go) - One thing to do just for ME during the season
💡
Why it works

It redefines "holiday magic" based on YOUR family values, not Instagram's. And it gives you permission to drop the stuff that's draining you — because burnt-out mom isn't magical for anyone.
