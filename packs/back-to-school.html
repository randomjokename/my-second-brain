Prompt #1
📋

The Supply List Decoder

The problem

You got "the list." It's 47 items long, half of them are brand-specific, and you need it done before next Tuesday. For three kids.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me organize this school supply shopping. Here are the supply lists: [paste the actual list(s)] Number of kids: [number] Budget goal: [amount] Stores I have access to: [Target, Walmart, Amazon, etc.] Give me: - A consolidated shopping list (combine duplicates across kids) - Items I probably already have at home - A store-by-store shopping plan to minimize trips - Generic alternatives for brand-specific items to save money - Total estimated cost
💡
Why it works

It turns a chaotic multi-list nightmare into one efficient shopping trip. And it catches the stuff you already own.

Prompt #2
📧

The "First Day" Teacher Email

The problem

You want to introduce yourself and your kid to the new teacher, mention important things they should know, but not be THAT parent. Fine line.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me write a beginning-of-year email to my child's new teacher. Child's name and grade: [name, grade] Teacher's name: [name] Important things to know: [learning differences, allergies, family situation, anxiety, etc.] Tone I want: [friendly but informative / brief / warm] What I want to avoid: [oversharing, sounding demanding, being too long] Keep it under [X] sentences. I want to seem like a supportive, reasonable parent — not one who's going to email every week.
💡
Why it works

It strikes that impossible balance between "here's what you need to know" and "I promise I'm not high-maintenance."

Prompt #3
🗓️

The Morning Routine Builder

The problem

Last year's mornings were chaos. This year you want a routine that actually works — for your kids AND your sanity. But every "morning routine chart" on Pinterest assumes your kids are tiny robots.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me build a realistic school morning routine. Number of kids and ages: [details] School start time: [time] Bus/drop-off time: [time] What makes mornings hard: [slow eater, can't find shoes, meltdowns, etc.] Non-negotiables: [breakfast together, making beds, etc.] Things I'd like to include if possible: [reading time, exercise, etc.] Build me a minute-by-minute timeline that: - Has buffer time built in - Accounts for the things that always go wrong - Includes what I should prep the night before - Is realistic for actual children, not Pinterest children
💡
Why it works

The "things that always go wrong" input is key. It plans around YOUR chaos, not some idealized morning.

Prompt #4
🍱

The Lunch Packing System

The problem

It's 10pm. You haven't packed lunches. You're out of ideas. Your kid will reject anything that touched another food. Every. Single. Night.

The prompt — copy & paste this
I need a school lunch system that doesn't make me want to cry. Number of kids: [number] Ages: [ages] Picky eating situation: [be honest — what will they actually eat?] Allergies/restrictions: [nut-free school, dairy-free, etc.] My prep willingness: [minimal/moderate/I'll batch-prep on Sunday] Budget: [per week roughly] Give me: - A 5-day lunch rotation I can repeat - A prep-ahead plan for Sunday - Substitution options for when I'm out of something - Snack ideas that aren't just goldfish crackers Keep it real. These are for actual children who will throw away anything "weird."
💡
Why it works

A rotating system means you only decide ONCE. Then it's autopilot.

Prompt #5
😰

The Anxiety Game Plan

The problem

Your kid is anxious about starting school — new teacher, new kids, new routine. You want to help but "you'll be fine!" isn't cutting it. And honestly, you're anxious too.

The prompt — copy & paste this
My [age]-year-old is anxious about [starting school / new school / new teacher / specific fear]. What they've said or shown: [describe the anxiety — what they say, behaviors you've noticed] What's worked before: [if anything] What makes it worse: [specific triggers] Our situation: [new school? Same school new grade? Any big changes?] Give me: - 3 things to say that actually validate their feelings (not "you'll be fine") - A simple prep activity we can do together before school starts - A morning-of script for when the anxiety peaks - A "code word" system idea for if they need help during the day - What to watch for that might mean they need more support
💡
Why it works

It goes beyond generic advice and gives you specific words and actions for YOUR kid's specific anxiety.

Prompt #6
📅

The After-School Schedule Tamer

The problem

Between homework, activities, playdates, and screen time negotiations, after-school hours are a free-for-all. Everyone's tired, hungry, and fighting. You need structure without becoming a drill sergeant.

The prompt — copy & paste this
Help me create an after-school routine that keeps everyone sane. Kids and ages: [details] School pickup time: [time] Activities this semester: [list with days/times] Homework situation: [amount, when it's due, kid's resistance level] Dinner time goal: [time] Bedtime: [time] The biggest after-school problem: [fighting, screen time battles, homework meltdowns, etc.] Build me a flexible after-school flow that includes: - Transition/decompression time (they need it) - Homework window - Activity logistics - Screen time boundaries that feel fair to them - Dinner prep I can do while they're occupied Don't make it rigid. Make it a flow, not a schedule.
💡
Why it works

"Flow, not schedule" is the key insight. Kids need structure AND flexibility, especially when they're tired.
